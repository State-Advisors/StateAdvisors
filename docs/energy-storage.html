Batteries

A battery stores power from the roof and gives it back when you want it. These are the reasons people add one.

Power after the sun is down

Panels make electricity while the sun is on them. A battery holds some of that power and gives it back in the evening, when the building is busy and the roof is quiet.

Chosen circuits stay on in an outage

A solar system tied to the grid shuts off when the grid goes down. That is a safety rule. A battery, with the equipment made for backup, can keep chosen circuits running: the refrigerator, a few lights, the internet, a well pump. How long they stay on depends on the size of the battery and what you leave switched on.

Your own power in the expensive hours

Some bills charge more in the late afternoon and evening. A battery can cover those hours with power the roof stored earlier, so you buy less at the high price. What that is worth depends on the rate at your address.

A reserve you set, and the rest for the day

You decide what the battery is for. It can keep a reserve for an outage and use the rest through an ordinary day. Backup, the evening bill, or both. The setting is part of the design, not a surprise after the install.

With the solar you have, or with solar you add

A battery can be added to a system that is already on, when the equipment allows it, or planned with a new array so the two are sized together. The roof, the battery, and the circuits you care about are one conversation.

What changes the size

How many hours of backup you want. Which circuits stay on. Whether the battery is mostly for the bill or mostly for outages. Those answers change the battery. An expert can walk through them for your place.
